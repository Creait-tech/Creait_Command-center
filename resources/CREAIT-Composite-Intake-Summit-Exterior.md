# CREAiT Growth & AI Diagnostic — Intake Responses
## ⚠ FICTIONAL COMPOSITE TEST CLIENT — for system validation & demo use
Client: Summit Exterior Services (Dana Brooks, Owner — she/her) · Marietta, GA · Exported for dry run

## Section 1 — You & the Business You Want
**Describe your company:** "We do commercial and residential exterior maintenance around metro Atlanta — landscaping contracts, pressure washing, gutter and roof maintenance. About 60% one-off residential and project work, 40 commercial properties on contracts. 15 people, 4 crews. Been at it 12 years."
**Primary objective:** Reliable revenue growth. Secondary: Reduce my dependence · Use AI & automation effectively.
**Three 12-month outcomes:** Revenue $2.4M → $3M · My weeks from 55 hrs → 40 · Quotes out same-day instead of "when I get to them."
**3-year vision:** "A $4M business run day-to-day by my ops manager, where I do strategy and the big commercial relationships. I want to take a real vacation. My daughter's graduation trip is in two summers and I want three weeks off without my phone."
**Current role:** Split across most of these. "Honestly I'm still the chief estimator, closer, and complaint department."
**Time & freedom:** 55 hrs/wk now · want 40 in 12 mo · 30 in 3 yrs · longest time away: 5 days (Thanksgiving) · **"What pulls you back in?"** — "Quotes. Nobody else can price a job. And the big property managers only want to talk to me."
**Q8 — biggest bottleneck (verbatim):** "We need more leads. Growth has been flat two years and I think marketing is the problem. We're invisible online compared to the bigger guys."

## Section 2 — Business Profile & Money
Founded 2014 · Dana 100% · Team: 12 FT, 3 PT, 1 ops manager (8 months in), 1 office manager, 3 crew leads.
Revenue model: one-time projects 45% · recurring contracts 28% · repeat-no-contract 22% · other 5%.
Customer mix: ~410 active · mixed B2B/B2C · **largest customer (Beacon Property Group) = 22% of revenue** · top five = 41% · **"revenue tied to my personal relationships: honestly 50%+ of commercial."**
Revenue: 2025: $2.41M · 2024: $2.36M · YTD on pace $2.45M. Trend: flat. "We lose as many as we win lately."
Gross margin: "around 38% I think — bounces between low 30s and mid 40s by month. Not tracked by job." Operating profit: ~$216K. Owner salary $110K + $40K distributions.
Cash: profitable but tight when big projects run · customer payment timing creates pressure ("commercial pays in 45–60 days").
Financial visibility: monthly but late ("QuickBooks reports around the 25th"), reviewed "when something looks off." Targets: revenue $3M, no others.

## Section 3 — The Growth Engine
Leads: referrals ~30/mo · Google/LSA ~25/mo ($1,400/mo) · property-manager RFPs 3–4/mo · door hangers seasonal. Quality tracked "in my head."
Lead response: "Office manager or I answer when we can — usually same day, sometimes next." Not in one system (voicemail, email, FB messages, LSA). No automated follow-up. "Maybe 40% get a second touch."
Conversion: ~35% of quotes close · not tracked by source · no defined process · most common loss reason: **"They went with someone who got them a number faster."**
Transaction value: residential job ~$1,850 · commercial contract ~$28K/yr · not tracked.
Retention: ~45% of residential repeats · commercial renewal ~85% · inactive customers not identified · never run reactivation. "We have 12 years of customers in QuickBooks we never talk to."
Pricing: unchanged 3 years except fuel surcharge · competitor-based · "Pricing is a current worry." Last real change: 2023.
Offers: no bundles, no maintenance plans for residential ("we've talked about it for years"), no premium tier.
Growth ownership: marketing = "me, when I have time" · sales = "me" · budget $1,400/mo LSA · numbers never reviewed together.
**20% jump test:** "Quality would slip and I'd be quoting at midnight. Crews could handle it — I couldn't."
Owner's guess (top 3): more leads · better website/SEO · maybe raise prices.

## Section 4 — Operations & Systems
Critical workflows: lead intake (unclear owner) · estimating/quoting (**Dana**) · scheduling (office mgr + ops mgr) · crew dispatch (crew leads) · job completion/QC (crew leads, informal) · invoicing (office mgr, after Dana approves) · collections (office mgr) · commercial renewals (**Dana**) · hiring (Dana + ops mgr).
Ownership: "handoffs are tribal knowledge." Documentation: ~15% documented, mostly outdated safety sheets.
Recurring failures: scheduling delays · incomplete job info to crews · late billing ("invoices go out 5–10 days after job — I'm the approval bottleneck") · slow collections · **"I have to step in personally" (checked)**. Biggest cost: late invoicing/collections.
Limiting resource: "My time." Evidence: "Every growth idea dies in my inbox."
Roles: "Things frequently fall back to me." KPIs: none weekly ("we look at revenue monthly"). Meetings: Monday crew huddle (scheduling only); no leadership meeting. Unresolved issues: "They come to me."

## Section 5 — Technology, AI & Automation
Systems: field-service app (scheduling/dispatch) · QuickBooks Online · Gmail/Sheets for quoting · no CRM · GroupMe for crews · no dashboards.
Integration: "Quote spreadsheet → field app → QuickBooks all manual. Office manager retypes everything." One person is the integration (checked).
Data: "Numbers conflict between the app and QuickBooks. I trust neither completely."
**Repetitive work (top 5):** quote assembly — Dana, ~15/wk, 30–40 min each (~8 hrs/wk) · invoice prep + chasing — office mgr, ~6 hrs/wk · schedule reshuffling — 4 hrs/wk · customer status calls/texts — ~5 hrs/wk across office · weekly numbers pull for Dana — 3 hrs/wk.
Automation today: appointment reminders in field app (reliable). That's it.
AI today: ops manager uses ChatGPT for emails. Nothing systematic.
Discipline: "None of these are consistently true." Tech risk: shared office login for QuickBooks · no tested backup of the quote sheets · Dana's phone holds all commercial contacts.
90-day readiness: budget "$10–15K if it clearly pays back" · Dana 4 hrs/wk · team 6 hrs/wk · appetite for change: high ("I'm done duct-taping").

## Section 6 — Owner Independence & Risk
Requires Dana: pricing exceptions · major sales · escalations · hiring/firing · purchasing >$500 · quality sign-off on commercial · banking/payments · vendor relationships · strategy. Hand off first: "quoting, invoicing approval, escalations."
**Four-week test:** "Week one, fine — crews run scheduled work. Week two, quoting stops, pipeline dries up, invoices pile up waiting on my approval. Week three, Beacon calls with an issue and nobody can make it right. Honestly by week four we'd have lost a commercial account and a month of new sales. Nobody else can access the bank or my contact list either."
Key-person risk: Dana (quoting, commercial relationships, banking) · Marcus, senior crew lead ("only one who knows the Beacon properties cold") · Beacon Property Group itself at 22%.
Contracts: commercial contracts written, "probably transferable, never checked" · licenses/insurance current · no continuity plan · company owns accounts "except everything is under my personal Google."
Transferability priorities: sales that don't need me · SOPs & knowledge transfer · leadership depth.
Readiness: "We can implement if priorities stay tightly limited." What could stop it: "Me. If it needs my hours, it won't happen. That's why nothing's changed in two years."
